Biogas power and biomethane upgrading

From pond to busbar, from raw biogas to boiler-grade gas. A biogas plant is a chain, not a machine; inlet gas quality decides whether you get 8,000 hours or 4,000 hours between overhauls.

Process chain

Six steps, one gas specification

CollectionCovered pond or CSTR, cover membrane, condensate collection, gas header.
BufferGas holder absorbs production and demand swings.
DesulphurisationBiological tower plus iron-oxide polishing.
ConditioningCooling, water and oil removal, boost to engine pressure.
PowerGas engine and generator, island or grid parallel, heat recovery.
UpgradingPSA, water scrubber or membrane to CH₄ ≥97%.
Sizing

Gas volume and engine capacity

At 60% methane and 38–40% electrical efficiency, one cubic metre of biogas yields about 2.2 kWh. Running below 70% load for long periods causes carbon build-up; choose a smaller unit or split into two.

Upgrading routes

PSA, water scrubber or membrane?

No universal answer — it depends on gas volume, purity, electricity price and footprint.

What 500 Nm³/h of raw gas yields

  • Raw biogas 500 Nm³/h at 60% CH₄
  • Methane input 300 Nm³/h, 97% recovery
  • 97% methane product ≈300 Nm³/h
  • Product heat ≈2,890 kW (LHV); upgrading power ≈125–150 kW

Guaranteed gas: CH₄ ≥97% (adjustable 96–99%), CO₂ ≤2–3%, H₂S ≤5 ppm, pressurised dew point ≤−40℃, O₂ ≤0.5%.

Power-only is rarely the best answer

If the site also needs steam, hot water or vehicle fuel, upgrading, CHP and BHP combined often pay back faster than power alone.
